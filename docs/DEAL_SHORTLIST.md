# Deal shortlist

The ASX control transactions I considered for this valuation, scored on the same seven criteria, and the deal I chose.

I reviewed twenty-four deals from ASX announcements, scheme booklets, target's statements and Takeovers Panel releases, accessed on 26 or 27 September 2026 (the access date is given with each source). Six failed a filter, I set aside four for other reasons, and I scored the remaining fourteen. The ten shortlisted deals come first, followed by the others and why I left them off.

## How the deals were scored

I applied two filters first: the bidder must have gained control (so terminated deals are out), and the target's equity value at the offer price must be at least about A$200m, so that listed comparables and analyst coverage exist. I scored the deals that passed from 1 (poor) to 5 (strong) on each criterion:

| Criterion | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 or 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 Completed | 100% acquired, confirmed by an ASX notice that was read | 100% acquired, confirmed only by a bidder release or a search summary | Bidder gained control, but the target is still listed | Failed or terminated |
| C2 IER public with range and method | Range, method and main inputs (discount rate or multiples) published as readable text | Range and method, with inputs partly missing or only in scanned pages | Range, with the method unclear | Range only |
| C3 Operating business, 3+ years of financials | Profitable, with comparable annual accounts | Profitable, but the history is disrupted by one-off items or a change of year end | Loss-making for much of the history | Developer with little revenue |
| C4 Listed comparables | Five or more close listed peers, including ASX names | Several peers, mostly offshore or in a different business mix | Few direct peers | No close listed peer |
| C5 Free data | Every input needed is in free documents | One input needs extra work (scanned pages, or forecasts not set out in the documents) | One material input needs judgement without free data | A central input is behind a paywall |
| C6 Story | A publicly announced rival proposal for control, a changed expert opinion, or a failed vote | Two of: a revised price, a supplementary or replacement expert report, a large move in the value of scrip consideration, a hostile bid, a pre-bid stake, a competing partial or proportional bid, Panel or court involvement | One of those features | Orderly single-bidder deal |
| C7 Interview use | Exercises DCF, trading comparables and precedent transactions on a mainstream business, plus deal mechanics | Useful, but one method dominates or the business is unusual | Sector-specific | Niche |

The total is out of 35. Ties are broken by C7, then by the more recent completion date.

## Notes on each deal

### 1. Domain Holdings Australia

CoStar bought 16.96% of Domain at A$4.20 on 20 and 21 February 2025, proposed A$4.20, then A$4.43 as "best and final" on 27 March 2025. The A$4.43 was paid as A$4.342 cash plus an 8.8 cent fully franked special dividend. It was a 42.0% premium to the undisturbed close of A$3.12 on 20 February 2025, and implied an enterprise value of about A$3.0bn. Nine Entertainment held about 60.05% and supported the deal. Grant Samuel valued Domain at A$4.06 to A$4.46 per share using DCF and capitalised EBITDA and EBITA, and found the scheme fair and reasonable; the offer sits near the top of that range. Year end 30 June; listed since 2017. Listed peers named in the booklet include REA Group, CAR Group and SEEK in Australia and Rightmove, Scout24 and Hemnet overseas. Scores: C6 is 4 for the pre-bid stake and the revised price, with no rival bidder. Sources: [Scheme Booklet, 30 June 2025, pp. 2, 11 to 17, 26, 45, 94 to 96](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20250630/pdf/06l8x6ntjgw6f7.pdf); [Implementation of scheme of arrangement, 27 August 2025](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20250827/pdf/06ndqqyt4sk7q9.pdf) (both accessed 27 September 2026).

### 2. Boral

Seven Group Holdings held 71.8% of Boral and bid for the rest in February 2024 with scrip plus cash, a 3.4% premium at announcement for the minimum consideration. Grant Samuel first found the offer not fair and not reasonable, "although the judgement is finely balanced", with a value of A$6.50 to A$7.13 per share. After SGH made A$1.70 cash unconditional and both companies paid franked dividends, Grant Samuel changed its opinion to not fair but reasonable, and the range cited became A$6.42 to A$7.05. SGH completed compulsory acquisition on 4 July 2024. Year end 30 June. Scores: C4 is 3 because the ASX building materials peers named in the target's statement, Adbri and CSR, were both acquired in 2024, leaving the US aggregates companies Martin Marietta and Vulcan Materials cited by the expert. Sources: [Target's Statement, 19 March 2024, pp. 6, 10, 16, 66 to 69](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20240319/pdf/061mx1h6k67t9n.pdf); [Second Supplementary Target's Statement, 12 April 2024](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20240412/pdf/062ftn1ssxyxr3.pdf); [SGH, compulsory acquisition completed, 4 July 2024](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20240704/pdf/0658tk9bw3hyx9.pdf) (all accessed 27 September 2026). Announced in February 2024, it qualifies on its completion date.

### 3. CSR

Saint-Gobain paid A$9.00 cash (a A$0.12 dividend plus A$8.88), a 33% premium to the A$6.77 close on 20 February 2024 and 12.4 times EBITDA. Kroll valued CSR at A$8.25 to A$9.26 per share as a sum of the parts: building products by DCF, CSR's effective 25.2% interest in the Tomago aluminium smelter by DCF, and property separately. Implemented 9 July 2024. Year end 31 March. Peers named by Kroll: GWA, Reliance Worldwide, Fletcher Building, James Hardie and Brickworks. Scores: C6 is 2, a single bidder and no revision. Sources: [Scheme Booklet, 26 April 2024, pp. 17 to 18, 121 to 122, 171 to 172](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20240426/pdf/062x9p8g3s3cxn.pdf); [Implementation of Scheme, 9 July 2024](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20240709/pdf/065f5jv75hwfl9.pdf) (both accessed 27 September 2026). Announced in February 2024, it qualifies on its completion date.

### 4. Insignia Financial

Bain Capital, CC Capital and Brookfield made eight non-binding proposals between them from December 2024, and the deed with CC Capital was signed on 22 July 2025 at A$4.80 cash, up from Bain's initial A$4.00. The price was a 56.9% premium to the A$3.06 close on 11 December 2024. Kroll valued Insignia at A$4.49 to A$5.08 per share by capitalising maintainable EBITDA of A$430m to A$450m at 8.5 to 9.0 times, and rejected DCF because the management model lacked a reasonable basis. Implemented 28 April 2026. Year end 30 June. Scores: C3 is 4 because remediation provisions and a class action settlement in FY25 disrupt the earnings history; C4 is 4 because the peers named in the booklet (AMP, Netwealth, HUB24, Perpetual and the UK's Quilter) have different business mixes; C6 is 5 for the rival sponsor proposals; C7 is 4 because Kroll relied on one method, so my DCF would have no published counterpart. Sources: [Scheme Booklet, 27 February 2026, pp. 9, 16, 92 to 95, 152 to 157](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260227/pdf/06wwkrzh14m716.pdf); [Scheme Implementation Deed announcement, 22 July 2025](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20250722/pdf/06m0fbt15sw7ny.pdf); [Implementation of Scheme, 28 April 2026](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260428/pdf/06yy61cscncb40.pdf) (all accessed 27 September 2026).

## My choice: Domain Holdings Australia

I chose Domain, the highest-scoring deal, on 27 September 2026. Grant Samuel used both DCF and capitalised earnings, so my DCF and my comparables can each be set against a published counterpart. It has close ASX-listed peers in REA Group, CAR Group and SEEK, plus overseas marketplaces such as Rightmove and Scout24. Every document is free, and Domain has published annual accounts with a 30 June year end since listing in 2017. I have not compiled precedent transactions yet; that is part of the data sourcing work.

The gap analysis has something specific to explain. The A$4.43 offer sits near the top of Grant Samuel's A$4.06–4.46 range, so my valuation has to show whether an independent range puts the price at the top, the middle or above. CoStar's 16.96% stake-build, the move to a "best and final" price and Nine's 60% holding raise deal questions beyond the numbers.

The weaknesses are a story with no rival bidder, and a narrow range (the top is about 10% above the bottom), so small assumption changes move the verdict. Whether Domain published audited FY25 accounts before it left the ASX is UNVERIFIED; if it did not, the latest audited year is FY24.

Deals I would have picked for a different emphasis:
- Insignia Financial, for a 2026 deal (implemented April 2026) and a private equity contest among three sponsors. Against it: Kroll rejected DCF, so my DCF would have no published counterpart, and remediation provisions complicate maintainable earnings.
- Boral, for the clearest valuation story: the expert changed its opinion from not fair and not reasonable to not fair but reasonable, the RG 111 distinction between the two tests. Against it: the scrip component must be valued, and the ASX peers were acquired in 2024.
- Pacific Smiles, for the best deal-tactics story (blocking stake, Panel declaration, failed scheme, two bids). Against it: no close listed peers, so the comparables method would be weak.

I did not choose Australian Strategic Materials. The documents are excellent and the story is strong, but the company is a developer, and an independent value would depend on one alloy-margin assumption that free data cannot support.
